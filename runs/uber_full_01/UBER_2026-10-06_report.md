# Financial & Strategic Glossary

- **Gross Bookings:** The total dollar value generated by transactions on Uber's platform, including taxes, tolls, and fees, before deducting driver and courier earnings.
- **Take Rate:** The percentage of Gross Bookings that Uber retains as GAAP net revenue after paying driver and courier partners.
- **Free Cash Flow (FCF):** Net cash provided by operating activities less purchases of property and equipment (capital expenditures).
- **Altman Z-Score:** A multi-factor financial formula measuring the probability of corporate bankruptcy; scores above 2.99 reside in the 'Safe Zone'.
- **Beneish M-Score:** An eight-metric forensic ratio model designed to detect earnings manipulation; scores below -1.78 indicate an unlikely manipulator.
- **Insurance Reserves:** Balance sheet accrued liabilities established to cover estimated current and future claims associated with vehicle accidents and commercial platform liability.
- **Uber One:** Uber's cross-platform subscription loyalty program providing discounts and benefits across rideshare and food delivery services.
- **EU Platform Work Directive:** European Union legislative framework establishing legal rebuttable presumptions regarding employee classification for digital platform gig workers.

# Layperson's Investment Overview: Uber Technologies, Inc.

### What Does Uber Do?
Uber operates the world's most widely recognized transportation and delivery platform. Through its smartphone application, it connects passengers needing rides with drivers (Mobility), consumers ordering food or groceries with local restaurants and couriers (Delivery), and commercial shippers with freight carriers (Freight).

### How Does Uber Make Money?
Every time someone books a ride or orders takeout, Uber collects the total payment, pays the driver or restaurant their share, and keeps a commission (called the 'take rate'). Because Uber does not own the vehicles or employ drivers as full-time salaried workers, its technology platform can serve millions of users with relatively low direct capital spending.

### Why Has the Business Improved?
For its first decade, Uber lost billions of dollars competing aggressively for market share. In recent years, management eliminated unprofitable side projects, raised pricing discipline, and scaled up advertising. As a result, Uber generated over $5.5 billion in GAAP operating profit in 2025 and produced more than $9.7 billion in free cash flow, allowing the company to repurchase billions of dollars of its own stock.

### What Are the Key Risks?
1. **Government Regulations:** Governments in Europe and the United States frequently debate whether gig drivers should receive mandatory employee benefits, which could raise Uber's labor expenses.
2. **Insurance Claims:** Uber must keep billions of dollars in reserve ($13.3 billion as of mid-2026) to pay accident liability claims.
3. **Self-Driving Cars:** As autonomous robotaxis enter city streets, Uber must partner with self-driving developers to ensure those fleets use Uber's app rather than competing with it directly.

### The Final Takeaway
Uber has evolved into an essential everyday utility with strong competitive advantages, expanding profits, and robust cash generation. At current market valuation levels, the stock offers an attractive long-term risk-adjusted opportunity, supporting a **BUY** recommendation.
